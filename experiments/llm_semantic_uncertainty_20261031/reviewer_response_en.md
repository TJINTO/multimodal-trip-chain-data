We added a supplementary repeated-sampling experiment to quantify variability in LLM-generated regional semantic profiles. Ten spatial units were selected from each of the four cities using population and POI features, without reference traffic observations. Each fixed input was processed ten times with Qwen3-8B at temperature 0.7 and top-p 0.9, using distinct reproducible seeds. All 400 outputs passed structured-field validation. The inputs, prompts, seed schedule, raw outputs, model identifiers, clustering evidence and summary results are available at https://github.com/TJINTO/multimodal-trip-chain-data/tree/main/experiments/llm_semantic_uncertainty_20261031.

Mean functional-label consistency was 85.5%. Bidirectional-NLI semantic entropy was 1.427 ± 0.391 nats across spatial units, indicating that free-text semantic descriptions retained meaningful variability. The mean coefficients of variation were 6.38% for the production multiplier, 10.50% for the attraction weight and 4.41% for the access-cost factor. When the sampled coefficients were propagated through the fixed exogenous-prior transformation, mean pairwise agreement CPC ranged from 0.9690 to 0.9916 across city-direction groups. Shanghai showed greater attraction-weight variation and lower label consistency than the other sampled cities.

These results quantify uncertainty rather than establish that it is absent. They suggest that the logarithmic transformation and normalization reduce the sensitivity of prior spatial shares to coefficient variation in the selected units. The agreement CPC compares repeated generated priors, not predictions against observed traffic. This supplementary Qwen3-8B elicitation study does not measure end-to-end PA-CVAE training variance or recreate the original LLM-authored ontology-generation session. The revised discussion should retain this scope and should not claim that low semantic entropy proves correctness or negligible full-pipeline variability.
